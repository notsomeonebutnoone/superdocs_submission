# Ten buyable use-case hypotheses

Research date: 2026-08-06. These are public-company research notes and company-level conversation-opener drafts. No one was contacted and no private individual is named. A public signal proves workflow relevance, not buying intent.

## 1. Greenhouse: safer offer-template maintenance

**Public signal:** Greenhouse Recruiting uses `.docx` offer templates with tokens. Replacing a template overwrites it for future offers and the prior version is no longer available in that workflow. [Source](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/6459061824155-Create-offer-document-template)

**Hypothesis:** Recruiting operations could make narrow clause or policy edits inside an existing offer template, review them, and export a styled DOCX for Greenhouse's current verification flow.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** Product partnerships or recruiting-operations product leadership; Greenhouse partner ecosystem, support community, and HR-technology events.

**Opener draft:** “Greenhouse already relies on carefully formatted Word offer templates. Could a reviewable, reversible editing layer help customers update offer language while preserving the existing template and tokens?”

**Validation risk:** Token and complex-Word fidelity need testing.

## 2. Loopio: maintain branded export themes

**Public signal:** Loopio Export Themes are DOCX files containing covers, styles, headers, footers, variables, and project content; customers send completed themes to Support for account setup. [Source](https://support.loopio.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038798673-How-Can-I-Have-an-Export-Theme-Added-to-My-Account)

**Hypothesis:** Users could request targeted changes to proposal language or theme content, review them in context, and export without recreating the branded file.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** Product partnerships or proposal-operations leadership; Loopio community, RFP-response events, and partner channels.

**Opener draft:** “Loopio's Export Themes already encode each customer's Word styles and dynamic variables. Could customers update those documents through reviewable edits while keeping the branded DOCX structure intact?”

**Validation risk:** Existing Loopio capabilities may overlap; variable preservation must be tested.

## 3. Responsive: move from insertion to document edits

**Public signal:** Responsive's Word integration searches and inserts recommended content and edits Word files stored in its Content Library; the company also advertises APIs and integrations. [Word integration](https://www.responsive.io/integrations/microsoft-word) · [Integrations](https://www.responsive.io/integrations/)

**Hypothesis:** Responsive could add instruction-driven edits to complete RFP sections, with review, revert, and style-preserved export.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** Product integrations or strategic partnerships; Responsive events, customer community, and proposal-management media.

**Opener draft:** “Responsive already brings approved knowledge into Word. Could the next step be reviewable, reversible edits to the proposal itself rather than stopping at content insertion?”

**Validation risk:** Native AI editing may already cover part of the job.

## 4. Clio: matter-specific edits after assembly

**Public signal:** Clio Draft converts Word files into reusable legal templates, autofills case information, and supports final review and version consistency. [Source](https://www.clio.com/draft/advanced-document-automation/)

**Hypothesis:** After assembly, a lawyer could request a narrow matter-specific change, review it, revert if needed, and export without losing firm formatting.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** Clio Draft product leadership or platform partnerships; Clio App Directory, Clio Cloud Conference, and legal-technology communities.

**Opener draft:** “Clio Draft already turns firms' Word documents into reusable templates. Could a reviewable editing layer help users make matter-specific revisions after assembly while preserving the firm's document structure?”

**Validation risk:** Legal accuracy, privilege, security, and overlap with Clio AI are material.

## 5. iManage: return an approved new revision

**Public signal:** iManage Work manages document revisions and co-authoring, integrates with Microsoft 365, and supports a technology-partner ecosystem. [Source](https://imanage.com/imanage-products/document-email-management/work/)

**Hypothesis:** A connector could retrieve a working document, apply narrow edits, collect approval, and return the styled DOCX as a new iManage revision.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** Technology alliances or document-AI product leadership; iManage partner network and legal-technology conferences.

**Opener draft:** “iManage already governs document revisions and Microsoft 365 collaboration. Could a partner integration add instruction-driven, reviewable edits and return the approved result as a new revision?”

**Validation risk:** Locking, security, API access, and native AI/MCP overlap require discovery.

## 6. Ironclad: execute scoped contract changes

**Public signal:** Ironclad's Word add-in connects documents to workflows and version history, synchronizes review changes, and supports branched versions. [Source](https://support.ironcladapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/29562007789079-Ironclad-for-Word-Add-In-Overview)

**Hypothesis:** SuperDocs could be an optional execution layer for narrowly scoped contract edits while approval and final version control remain in Ironclad.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** Platform partnerships or workflow product leadership; Ironclad community, State of Digital Contracting programming, and legal-operations events.

**Opener draft:** “Ironclad already connects Word editing and version history to contract workflows. Could an API-driven layer execute narrowly scoped document changes while keeping approval inside Ironclad?”

**Validation risk:** High native-product overlap means a specific gap must be proven.

## 7. M-Files: edit before check-in

**Public signal:** File or metadata changes create new M-Files object versions, and M-Files exposes web-service and application extensibility. [Objects and versions](https://developer.m-files.com/Getting-Started/Objects-And-Versions/) · [Developer portal](https://developer.m-files.com/)

**Hypothesis:** An extension could check out a Word document, apply and review targeted edits, then check the result in as a new object version.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** Product partnerships or solution engineering; M-Files partner ecosystem, developer portal, and information-management events.

**Opener draft:** “M-Files already creates an auditable object version whenever a document changes. Could an extension add reviewable AI edits before the revised Word file is checked back in?”

**Validation risk:** Authentication, checkout rules, and enterprise-file fidelity need testing.

## 8. Procore: revise narrative project documents

**Public signal:** Procore centralizes construction documents, tracks revisions, controls permissions, and operates an integration marketplace. [Source](https://www.procore.com/platform/document-management)

**Hypothesis:** Teams could revise Word-based scopes, RFIs, method statements, and handover documents, then return an approved styled revision to Procore.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** App-marketplace partnerships or document-management product leadership; Procore Marketplace, Groundbreak, and construction-technology communities.

**Opener draft:** “Procore already governs project-document permissions and revisions. Could a marketplace integration help teams make reviewable edits to Word-based project documents and return an approved revision?”

**Validation risk:** The actual share of DOCX-heavy workflows and revision-upload API support is unknown.

## 9. Highspot: governed account personalization

**Public signal:** Highspot manages governed sales content, approval workflows, publishing rules, and outdated-content updates, and advertises an integration ecosystem. [Content management](https://www.highspot.com/product/content-management/) · [Marketplace](https://www.highspot.com/marketplace/)

**Hypothesis:** Sellers could adapt approved Word collateral for an account, review changes against governance rules, and export without creating an unmanaged copy.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** Marketplace partnerships or sales-content product leadership; Highspot Marketplace, Spark events, and sales-enablement communities.

**Opener draft:** “Highspot already governs which content is current and available to sellers. Could a connected editor allow controlled changes to approved Word collateral without losing its design?”

**Validation risk:** Document formats and overlap with native AutoDocs/AI are unclear.

## 10. PandaDoc: an upstream DOCX editing step

**Public signal:** PandaDoc exposes APIs for document generation, management, and signing, supports embedded editing and templates, and publishes an MCP server. [Source](https://developers.pandadoc.com/)

**Hypothesis:** For workflows beginning with a styled Word file, teams could review targeted edits in SuperDocs before passing the result into PandaDoc's transaction and signing flow.

**Buyer / listening hypothesis:** Developer-platform partnerships or document-workflow product leadership; PandaDoc developer channels, marketplace, and sales-operations communities.

**Opener draft:** “PandaDoc already provides APIs for generation, management, and signing. Is there room upstream for reviewable changes inside an existing styled Word document before it enters the transaction flow?”

**Validation risk:** Native editing overlap and DOCX-model interoperability must be proven.

## Prioritization

Start discovery with Greenhouse, Loopio, M-Files, Procore, and Responsive because the public workflow and complementary integration point are clearest. Treat Clio, iManage, Ironclad, Highspot, and PandaDoc as higher-overlap hypotheses. These openers remain drafts until SuperDocs authorizes outreach.
